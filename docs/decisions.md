## Standing principles

Constraints that hold across every decision below, and outrank them.

### P1 — Final judgement is always a human

K, 2026-08-11. An invariant, not a phase.

No eval, no judge agent, and no accumulation of green checks constitutes
acceptance. Automated gates exist to make a human's judgement cheaper and
better-evidenced, never to replace it.

- Deterministic gates can **reject**. Nothing can **accept**.
- A fully green run means *ready for review*, not *approved*.
- [D14](#d14--the-production-bar)'s sign-off is Andrew's and stays Andrew's.
  Passing evals does not satisfy it and does not create a default of approval.
- No eval result may be used to argue that review can be skipped, sampled or
  deferred — including by us, when the suite is working well and the temptation
  is strongest.

Holds regardless of how good the evals become. Recorded in
ADR 0006.

### P2 — Nothing is permanent until it is

K, 2026-08-12. A standing principle, and it outranks the register below it.

We are shaping a solution. **Every decision in this document is provisional until
something is shipped that depends on it**, and the point of recording decisions is
not to stop revisiting them — it is to make revisiting them cheap and honest.

- **A recorded decision is not a settled one.** It is a position with its
  reasoning attached, so that a later reversal can be argued against evidence
  rather than against memory.
- **Being critical of the system is the work, not an interruption to it.** A
  challenge to a decision taken an hour ago is not churn; churn is discovering
  the flaw after something depends on it.
- **The test of a decision is whether we can still defend it later**, when the
  reasoning has gone cold and the evidence has moved. That is why the reasoning
  is recorded and not just the outcome.
- **Every decision should state what would reverse it.** D29 does — "a negative
  result reopens this row". Most do not, and that is a defect in this document
  rather than a property of the decisions.
- **Confidence comes from surviving attack, not from being written down.**

This is why round 5's verdict was reassessed rather than defended, why G50 was
narrowed by the agent that raised it, and why an ADR taken today was corrected
the same day. Those are the register working, not the register failing.

### P3 — Adopt the standard. Do not invent beside it.

K, 2026-08-12. A standing principle, and it outranks the register below it.

**Using the web is the point, not a compromise.** Its value is the systems and
standards that already exist there — HTML semantics, ARIA, CSS custom
properties, established component conventions. Every place this format states an
opinion that a standard already covers, we have taken on a translation layer
nobody asked for and made the output worse, not more portable.

- **A gap in our vocabulary is not automatically a gap to fill.** Ask first
  whether a standard already says it. G58 was filed as roles to add;
  the correct reading was that atoms do not belong in this format at all.
- **Adopt, do not rebuild.** The founding memo said this about primitive
  libraries, ADR 0007 reversed it
  without evidence, and every measurement since has favoured the memo — G56,
  G57, G58.
- **Opinions cost twice**: once to specify, once to keep true on every target.
- **The test is the project's goal**, not the format's completeness. A format
  that expresses everything and ships nothing has failed.

This does not license under-specification. Where a standard is genuinely absent
or diverges between engines — G53 is the measured case — that is
exactly where this format earns its keep.

## What would reverse each decision

Backfilled 2026-08-12 under [P2](#p2--nothing-is-permanent-until-it-is), which asks
every decision to state what would reopen it.

D29 is the model: it names **an observation**, **who or what produces it**, and
**what it reverses**. That shape is the bar here. Where no honest condition is
available the row says so — a fabricated one is worse than an absent one, because
it implies a test nobody intends to run.

**29 of 32 settled decisions now carry a condition.** Three do not, each for a
different and stated reason. P1 carries none by construction, which is a real
category and is recorded as such rather than left looking like an oversight.

### Three findings first, because they are the point of the exercise

**1. One obligation is already discharged. D30 is satisfied on iOS.** The
re-measure at the OS 26 floor has happened: commit `f6948e5`, *"D30: re-measure at
the OS 26 floor — G32 reproduces"*, landed the destination pin
(`tools/ios-tree/measure.sh:25`, `iPhone 17 Pro, OS=26.1`) and the regenerated
goldens in the same commit. G32 reproduces at 26 rather than dissolving, so the
finding D30 was written to protect survives its own re-measurement. **iOS only** —
Android, macOS, Windows and Linux are not discharged.

**2. The crux O2 was reframed around is partly answered already, and the answer is
sitting in the repository.** [O2](#open) now turns on one unmeasured fact —
*does a screen-reader user reach the subtitle on a wrapped native merged unit?*
Half of it is on disk. At the OS 26 floor,
`tools/ios-tree/goldens/round-5-ios-contentcard.raw.txt:13-14` shows the merged
unit **retaining both `StaticText` nodes**, title and subtitle, in the platform
accessibility hierarchy; the conformant web arm
(`tools/linux-tree/measurements/tauri/arm-stretched-merged.derived.tree`) contains
no text nodes at all. What is **not** answered is whether those retained nodes are
independently reachable — `tools/ios-tree/goldens/README.md:30-35` states plainly
that the stop claim "is reasoned, not measured." **The gap between a decided O2 and
an undecided one is one instrument flag**, not a new programme.

**3. Four rows reduce to the same missing measurement, and it has now been taken.**
D9, D18, D19 and D23 all reverse on cross-run divergence of the accessibility
tree. The noise floor was measured 2026-08-13 across **three engines**
(WebKitGTK, WKWebView, macOS WebKit): the unpinned floor is zero on WebKitGTK
and noisy on the two Apple WebKits (the G53 title leak, D2), and the
pinned arm (D52) converges to 2 stops on all three. D9/D18/D19 now carry the
measured floor. **D23's reversal is superseded, not blocked** — it presumed a
generated primitive tier with a spec, but D39/D41/D50 made the atom tier
adopted (Ark UI), so there is no primitive spec to run N agents against; the
composition half of D23 stands, resting on the measured floor. The floor is
engine-specific; pinning is what makes convergence cross-engine.

### D21 — No platform personas until evals exist

K, 2026-08-11. Deferred, not rejected.

Platform-specific agent personas and skills — an iOS specialist, an Android
specialist — would plausibly raise output quality. Round 4 produced three craft
failures a persona would likely have caught: a 40dp touch target under Android's
48dp minimum, the `Button`-wrapping-`Menu` structure that silently swallows a
control from VoiceOver, and a macOS right-click menu placed on a photograph.

**They wait for the eval suite (`evals.md`).** Two reasons.

**1. We cannot currently measure whether they help.** Decision counts are
disqualified in `ledger.md` — ±40% run-to-run. The accessibility-tree metric that
would detect a real quality change does not exist yet. Adopting personas now
means adopting on judgement and never knowing, which is the failure pattern this
project has already hit repeatedly.

**2. Personas contaminate the falsification rounds.** Those rounds answer *can
any competent engineer build this from the spec alone?* — which only means
something if the implementer is generic. Three agents sharing an iOS persona may
converge because of the persona rather than the format. That is precisely the
error round 3 (`round-3-verdict.md`) caught, where convergence was attributed to
`a11y.mode: "exclude"` and the judge traced it to lowering §A instead. A persona
is a much larger shared input than one paragraph.

### When they do arrive: skill in the persona, decisions in the lowering document

A persona carries **craft** — SwiftUI idiom, Compose semantics, HIG knowledge,
which APIs are traps. The `swifty`, `swift-design`, `swiftui-patterns` and
`apple-patterns-check` skills are already that shape.

A persona must **not** carry decisions — header heights, overflow placement,
role-to-token mappings. Those stay in `lowering/`, because
lowering documents are versioned, diffable and reviewable line by line. When the
round-2c iOS header failed to collapse we could point at the exact line and fix
it in one commit. The same defect inside a persona prompt would have been
invisible.

Given this project has produced **eight contradictions across three hand-written
binding documents**, moving decisions into a less inspectable container is the
wrong direction.

### What generic agents bought

Worth stating because it is easy to undersell: the reason four rounds of evidence
are trustworthy is that no implementer had context. That property should be
preserved deliberately in the falsification rounds even once personas run
everywhere else.

**And one reason it did not originally have, which is now the strongest:**

An unstable format is far cheaper under build time. A format change means
regenerating committed source. Under runtime it means every deployed renderer
staying backward-compatible with every document version in the wild — the
documented Adaptive Cards failure mode, where Teams mobile supports schema ≤1.2
and later cards "might not render correctly." You can regenerate a file; you
cannot regenerate a renderer already installed on a user's machine.

The spec is 0.2, carries 14 gaps from one round, and has a base-type redesign
pending that touches every node. That instability is an argument for build time,
not a reason to defer the decision.

**Also relevant:** D14's production bar requires an accessibility audit and
snapshot coverage. Under runtime, "which renderer version produced this" becomes
a variable in every audit.

**What this does not settle:** O2 — toolkit count. Binding time and toolkit count
are independent axes, and toolkit count is the one that dominates cost. The
cheapest configuration on the board (CMP + web at 0.60) is build-time, so nothing
here forecloses it.

### D15 — Figma is not the authoring surface

Andrew, 2026-08-11, in his own words: *"I don't think Figma is really the right
move… I think this replaces Figma in a lot of ways."* He likes Figma's UI for
style guides and variables, but holds that this can be documented instead.

His own spike is the pipeline in miniature: **tokens as JSON → a script → CSS →
a rendered HTML style guide**, with the JSON as source of truth. Authoring is
conversational — *"I just chat with Claude to tell it what I want."*

Three consequences:

1. **This validates ADR 0003 from the
   strongest possible direction.** The designer chose text-first unprompted,
   rather than being argued into it.
2. **The Figma extraction study (`research/figma-extraction.md`) becomes evidence
   the call is cheap.** Only 20–30% of a UCS document was ever extractable, and
   roles, all behaviour and all bindings were structurally impossible. Dropping
   Figma forfeits the easy structural third and none of the hard part.
3. **It resolves O3.** With no Figma in the path there is no extraction step and
   no human-maintained mapping table.

**Risk not yet tested:** Andrew is one designer on a spike. Replacing Figma for a
design org of one is a different proposition from client work, where Figma is
often the shared artefact. Not raised with him yet.

### D16 — Pull the linter forward

K, 2026-08-11. The linter was step 3, gated behind the falsification test. That

That changes what the schema and linter are *for*. They stop being hygiene and
